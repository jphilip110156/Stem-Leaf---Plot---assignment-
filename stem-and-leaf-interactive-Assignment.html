Stem-and-Leaf Plots

Learn how to organize numerical data while keeping every original value. Then build your own plots with new randomized data.

1. What is a stem-and-leaf plot?

A stem-and-leaf plot separates each number into two parts:

  • Stem: the leading digit or digits.
  • Leaf: the final digit.

Key: 4 | 7 means 47

For decimal practice, the key will show the correct place value.

Key: 2 | 1 means 21. Notice that leaves are written in increasing order and repeated values are kept.

3. Follow the steps

  1. Arrange the data from least to greatest.
  2. Write all consecutive stems from the smallest stem to the largest stem.
  3. If a stem has no data values, keep that row and leave its leaf space blank. This shows the true spread of the data.
  4. Write the final digit as a leaf beside its stem.
  5. Order the leaves from least to greatest and include a key.

4. Build the plot

Select a level. Type every consecutive stem and its leaves. Separate leaves with spaces (for example, 1 4 4 7). If a stem has no leaves, enter the stem and leave the leaf box blank.
